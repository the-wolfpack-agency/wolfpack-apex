## Conversational Context (the part git can't see)

### The pattern that produced most of the day

Fifteen or so real defects, and the overwhelming majority were invisible to code
review and to the suite. They surfaced when something was actually run:

- Walking Cognito showed "3 distinct forms" on a system with fifteen. `harvestSurface`
  selected `form` elements, and the forms are built in a canvas editor that renders
  inputs inside plain divs. Would have missed the fields on most client systems.
- `connectedSystems` queried a `vendor` column that does not exist. Every lookup threw,
  the fail-closed path swallowed it, and the capability menu was correct by accident.
- Three integrations reported success while the machine had no Microsoft connection.
  Presence and mailbox settings map failure to null, so reading the return value
  recorded mailbox settings as working while its scope is not even requested.
- The Microsoft sync layer, five workers and a dispatcher, had never been called.
- Query expansion, fully built and evaluated, was never supplied to the assistant.
- Eleven learning extractors looked unwired and were actually starved.
- "What are the payment terms in our SOW" was listed as the top content gap and
  already answered.

### Three numbers that looked plausible and were wrong

Each would have rendered beautifully on a dashboard.

**5,624 meeting hours.** Thirty OOO entries and sixty-four trips held ninety per cent
of the calendar. Real figure: 547. Found because somebody said an OOO entry is a
vacation day. Nothing in the data says that.

**12.6 days away, identical for all six people.** Every OOO entry sits on all six
calendars because the team invites everybody. That was everyone's holidays counted six
times, and it looked entirely reasonable.

**One document answering 754 questions.** Took four passes to kill. Excluding
self-identifying harnesses left 710. Then 609 of those were two identities on two days:
automated runs issued account ids, so nothing about WHO was asking could catch them.
Removing the busiest sitting passed the very case it was written for. Counting how few
sittings hold half then called everything a burst. What works is WHEN the heavy days
fall: a habit's are scattered, a burst's are adjacent.

### Mistakes I made that are worth not repeating

- Reported tests passing twice when they had never run. The jest pattern contained
  `(dashboard)`, which is a regex group, so it matched nothing and the green result came
  from a different directory.
- Built the empty-versus-failed collapse into the integrations check, on the day spent
  fixing instances of it elsewhere.
- Said the calendar sync would light up `calendar-signals`. It does not: that extractor
  reads `instinct_calendar_events_written`, a log of writes the product itself made.
  My source mapping was wrong for four of nine extractors.
- A doc comment containing `*/` closed the comment. Twice.

### Decisions worth keeping

- **An insight ships once it has survived being wrong at least once.** Three did not
  survive the first pass.
- **Client conventions are configuration, never a longer built-in list.** "Demo" is a
  test drive at a dealership and a sales meeting everywhere else.
- **Concentration is the signature of a convention nobody has mentioned.** Detecting
  that a few entries dominate finds a client's quirk without knowing what it is. It
  asks and never concludes.
- **Counts and kinds, never values**, for anything describing sensitive material.
- **Calendar first, mail off.** Caching a mailbox is a decision with an owner, not a
  default that arrives because the code was written.

## Known Blockers

- **The eval pairs file is not in the repo.** Retrieval changes cannot be graded, only
  argued about. Twelve harvested pairs need review to become a set.
- **`eval-retrieval` and the embedder disagree** about whether embeddings are configured
  locally: the eval checks `AZURE_OPENAI_EMBEDDING_DEPLOYMENT`, the embedder finds its
  config another way and works. Small defect, blocks local grading.
- **Mailbox settings cannot reach 18 of 18.** `MailboxSettings.Read` needs administrator
  consent and is deliberately not requested. It is one of four decisions in the client
  access pack, not a bug.
- **Demo grounding needs the client's libraries.** Two of three guide examples ask about
  documents a Porsche academy corpus does not hold. Choosing replacements needs their
  document list, and using ours would recreate the failure at their site.
- **59 of our own documents hold a never-send value** (60 card numbers, 43 API keys,
  4 IBANs). The boundary stops them. Who may quote which document is a decision nobody
  has made yet.
