## Headline

The assistant stopped being a place to ask one question at a time: it now runs
chains of the tools somebody already uses, stops where only a person can decide,
measures what that person's own steps cost them, and repairs itself when a step
breaks.

## Conversational Context (the part that matters tomorrow)

**The product thesis, in the CTO's words.** Middleware for a company's existing
tools, legacy systems and processes, reached through one contextual UI. It uses
AI only where judgement is genuinely required and otherwise just operates the
tooling from a single point. The differentiator is not teaching non-engineers to
build agents; it is reducing the friction between a human and the software they
already use. "The human is absolutely the priority here."

**Why the human step is the asset.** There is a ceiling on how much software one
person touches in a day, and what makes it feel large is that the PERSON is the
integration layer, carrying context between windows. A chain that pauses records
the boundary between machine work and human work, which nothing else in the
estate knows. That measurement is the product, not a by-product.

**Decisions made verbally, with reasons.**

- **No drag-and-drop canvas.** Proposed, then ruled out by the CTO the same
  session: "we must keep this as simple as possible and that doesn't simplify."
  A canvas re-introduces the learning curve other tools are hung up on. Editing
  ships as a LIST instead (remove, move, replace), each operation a sentence
  somebody could say out loud.
- **No demo mode, no fixture connector.** A fixture connector was built and
  DELETED unmerged after the CTO said "I want a working tool, not a demo". What
  was wanted was a walkthrough of real functionality. Do not rebuild it.
- **Templates, not seeded data.** The agreed way to prove the product before a
  client connects anything is the checked template library: it says which
  workflows will run in THIS workspace and which need something connected.
- **Client shape.** Most likely a corporate auto client, otherwise retail. That
  decision drove the router's content-policy profiles.

**A correction worth keeping.** I named connector self-onboarding as the top
readiness gap. It was already built (add credentials plus a live verify button
at /admin/connectors). Verify before claiming a gap.

**Where the CTO wants to go next**, stated but not built: an agent inside the
auto DMS tool that connects every area of that product through one chat, and the
assistant eventually holding context across Instinct, auto and Beyond at once.
Both were explicitly deferred.

## Open Items / What's Next

1. **Verification against the deployed URL.** Everything is green locally and in
   CI and NOTHING in this arc has been checked on wolfpack-instinct.vercel.app
   while signed in. This is the one item that needs a credential rather than
   code: ADMIN_E2E_EMAIL / ADMIN_E2E_PASSWORD are still not provisioned.
2. **Sharing a routine with a team.** Chains are per person today, which is how
   adoption spreads inside a company and the last item from the readiness list
   that was not built.
3. **The gap counter is the roadmap.** plan_my_day records every described step
   with no tool behind it, and routine_templates records every template a
   workspace is shown but cannot use. After a few weeks those two counts are a
   ranked build queue made of real work from real people. Read them before the
   next planning discussion.
4. **A human step cannot yet be scheduled independently of its routine.** "The
   day before, prep the brief" works at routine granularity; a standalone
   reminder at a time does not exist.

## Known Blockers

- **Signed-in production pages remain unverifiable.** No ADMIN_E2E_EMAIL /
  ADMIN_E2E_PASSWORD, so every e2e that needs a session runs against a local
  server. The directive says code that passes locally and blanks in production
  is a failed task; this is the standing exception and it needs a credential.
- **The e2e suite reuses whatever is on port 3000.** During this session that
  was a different product's dev server, and the specs silently ran against the
  wrong application until the page dump showed a Porsche login. If you run them
  locally, either free the port or pass PROD_URL=http://localhost:<port> at a
  port you started Instinct on.
