## Conversational Context (the part that matters tomorrow)

**The M365 incident (this is the load-bearing memory).** Ashley (operations teammate, regular role) couldn't connect Microsoft 365 to Instinct. She hit Microsoft's "Need admin approval" page no matter what we did. We spent over an hour theorizing about Microsoft tenant configuration: checked the right Azure app was being used, checked Vercel env vars, checked admin consent dialog checkboxes, used the tenant-pinned `/adminconsent` URL, granted tenant-wide consent on `cb670432-653d-4195-b826-070380d83e99` in tenant `e8c58440-91b0-4ddc-bdb1-e33ab22986b8`, added Ashley to Enterprise Application Users-and-Groups. Nothing worked. The CTO (Nick) connected fine through every test we ran.

The actual bug was in our own code: `prompt=consent` hard-coded in both `getSigninAuthUrl` and `getAuthUrl` at [src/lib/microsoft-graph.ts](src/lib/microsoft-graph.ts). That parameter forces Microsoft to bypass cached tenant admin consent on every sign-in. For admins (Nick), the per-user re-consent succeeds. For non-admins (Ashley), the re-consent fails because she lacks personal authority to grant admin-only scopes like `Files.ReadWrite.All` — landing her on "Need admin approval" *even though tenant admin consent was already in place*. Two-line fix in PR #99 (`prompt=select_account`). The whole class of failure was self-inflicted.

This cost Ashley five-plus failed connect attempts and made the CTO look bad to his team. Save the lesson: when a third-party-platform symptom persists past two obvious fixes, grep our own code that calls that service BEFORE theorizing more about the third party. Memory captured at `feedback_read_our_own_code_first_on_third_party_bugs.md` and `feedback_oauth_admin_consent_tenant_url.md`.
