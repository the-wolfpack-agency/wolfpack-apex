# Outbound cadence (the weekly system)

A repeatable, founder-led outbound motion the seller can run every week without
reinventing it each time. The goal is one metric: booked scans. Everything here
serves that. Pairs with founder-gtm-playbook.md (the personal motion and
war-story bank), outreach-and-deck.md (the email library), and
pitch-kit.md (the language). This doc is the operating system; those are the
content. No em dashes. Never name a competitor. Every template is honest and
specific, no hype.

---

## The one CTA, every channel

The free read-only shadow-AI scan is the primary call to action everywhere. Cold
email, LinkedIn, warm intro, follow-up, breakup note: the ask is always a
20-minute scan readout, never "buy" and never "let me send a deck." Booking the
scan is the win. If a message does not move toward a booked scan, cut it.

---

## Step 1: Build the named-account list

Do this once, then top it up weekly. Build in the four buckets from
founder-gtm-playbook.md (Blue Bite alumni and brand contacts, consulting-era
Fortune 500 contacts, Wolfpack relationships, cold-but-high-fit), and tier every
account by fit:

- **Tier 1:** deploying AI agents in production now. Work these first.
- **Tier 2:** shipping AI features or AI-written code.
- **Tier 3:** "exploring AI." Last.

Track in a sheet or light CRM with these columns: account, contact, role, bucket
(warm or cold), tier, trigger (see step 2), stage, last touch, next touch. A clean
list is a forecast.

---

## Step 2: A trigger-based hook per account

Cold-but-relevant beats cold. Before you write to an account, find one public
signal that proves they have the problem, and open with it. This makes the message
about them, not us.

Where to find the trigger:
- A public AI product launch, agent feature, or "we built an AI assistant" post.
- Job posts for AI, ML, platform, or agent engineers (they are scaling AI surface).
- A funding round or growth news (budget plus pressure to ship AI fast).
- A public security or compliance commitment (SOC2, ISO 42001, EU exposure).
- A conference talk or blog post about their AI architecture.

The hook formula: their public signal, plus the question it raises, plus the scan.

> "Saw [specific public AI launch or job post]. As that ships, the question your
> security team will ask is what those AI surfaces are allowed to do and whether
> you can prove what they did. We can show you that in a free 20-minute scan."

No trigger? Drop the account to the bottom of the queue. A message with a real
trigger outperforms ten generic ones.

---

## Step 3: The multi-touch sequence

One account gets a sequence, not a single shot. Most replies come on touch 2 to 4.
Space them: Day 0, Day 3, Day 7, Day 14. Mix channels. Warm intro always replaces
touch 1 when available.

### Touch 1, Day 0: trigger email (or warm intro)
Subject: your ungoverned AI, in a 20-minute readout

> Hi [name],
> Saw [specific trigger]. As you ship that, the AI is taking actions across your
> systems, and most teams cannot answer two questions: what is the AI allowed to
> do, and can you prove what it did.
> We built the authorization gate and record for exactly that. The fastest way to
> show value is a free, read-only scan of one repo, you will see your own
> ungoverned AI surface and any leaked keys in a few minutes.
> Worth 20 minutes this week?
> [name]

### Touch 2, Day 3: LinkedIn connect plus note
> "Building the authorization gate and record for AI actions. Saw [trigger] at
> [company], would value your view on how you are governing what those AI surfaces
> are allowed to do."
Then, after they accept: the one hook plus "free read-only scan of one repo,
20-minute readout, no commitment."

### Touch 3, Day 7: the proof-forward nudge
Subject: a sample of what the scan shows

> Hi [name],
> Following up. Rather than describe it, here is a sanitized example of the
> governance and compliance summary the scan produces: [link to /governance-sample].
> Yours would show your real AI surfaces and your ungoverned count. If you can get
> me read access to one repo, I will send your version by [day].
> [name]

### Touch 4, Day 14: the breakup note
> Hi [name],
> Sounds like the timing is not right, so I will close this out. If governing what
> your AI is allowed to do, and proving what it did, moves up the list, I am one
> reply away and the scan is still free.
> [name]

Breakup notes get more replies than nagging. Send it, mean it, and move on.

---

## Step 4: Qualification criteria

Not every reply is a real opportunity. Qualify fast so you spend time on Tier 1.
A prospect is qualified to book a scan when most of these are true:

- They are deploying AI agents or action-taking AI features in or near production.
- There is no clear owner of what those agents are allowed to do today.
- They cannot confidently prove what their AI did last month.
- There is a forcing function: a stalled deal, an audit, a compliance push, or a
  recent incident or near-miss.
- The contact can grant read access to one repo, or can pull in the person who can.

Two or more true: push for the scan. Mostly false: nurture, do not chase.

## The weekly cadence checklist

Run this every week. It is a system, not a vibe. Targets are from
founder-gtm-playbook.md so the two stay consistent.

- [ ] Add or refresh 10 named accounts with a real trigger each.
- [ ] Send 10 outreach touches (warm intros first, then triggered cold).
- [ ] Advance every account in the pipeline one stage, or mark it dead.
- [ ] Follow up on every prior touch on the Day 3 / 7 / 14 schedule.
- [ ] Run toward 3 discovery calls.
- [ ] Book 2 scans (the only metric that counts).
- [ ] Log every reply line that worked, to feed back into the templates.
- [ ] Send the breakup note to anything silent past Day 14.

Pipeline stages to track (same as the playbook): Contacted, Replied, Call booked,
Demo done, Scan booked, Scan delivered, Pilot proposed, Closed.

---

## How to update this doc

After the first 20 to 30 touches, replace these templates with the exact lines
that got replies, and write down which trigger types converted best. The CTA (the
free scan) and the weekly checklist change least; the templates and the
trigger-source list change most. When a template here improves, update
outreach-and-deck.md to match so the seller has one library, not two.
