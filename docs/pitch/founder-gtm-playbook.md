# Founder GTM playbook (for Nick)

A personalized system for reaching clients, built around who you actually are: a
Head-of-Engineering-through-acquisition who shipped for adidas, eBay, Moet
Hennessy, and Bvlgari at Blue Bite, then ran Fortune 500 consulting engagements,
now the founding tech hire at the Wolfpack Agency. Your gap is the motion, not the
credibility. This closes it.

No em dashes (house style). Pairs with demo-runbook.md, outreach-and-deck.md, and
pitch-kit.md.

---

## 0. The reframe (read this first, twice)

You are not an engineer who is bad at sales. You are a credible peer selling to
peers, which is the highest-trust position in enterprise. Your buyers are CISOs
and VP-Engs at exactly the kind of companies you have already built for. They
screen out slick salespeople and lean toward someone who has been in the trench.

And you already know consultative selling. You ran Fortune 500 engagements:
diagnose the problem, prescribe the solution, scope the work. The OGIAM sale is
the same motion, pointed at a new target, where the prescription is your product.
You are not learning a new skill. You are aiming an existing one.

The only two things to internalize:
1. Lead with their pain, not your product depth. (Your instinct is to go deep.
   Resist it until they ask "how.")
2. The next step is always small: "let me run a scan," never "sign here."

## 1. Your 60-second credibility opener

Use this in the first minute of any call, so the rest of the conversation runs on
trust. Tighten it to your voice, but keep the spine.

> "Quick context on why I'm building this. I spent years as head of engineering at
> Blue Bite shipping products for adidas, eBay, Moet Hennessy, Bvlgari, through to
> an acquisition, then ran Fortune 500 engagements at consulting groups. I've been
> the person at 3am when a region went dark, when a bad deploy took down an
> environment, when we stood up AI ontologies for enterprise teams and realized
> the governance layer was missing. OGIAM is that missing layer. So this is built
> from having lived the problem, not theorized it."

Why it works: brand logos buy 30 seconds of attention, the war scars buy trust,
and "lived it, not theorized it" pre-empts the "is this real" question.

## 2. Your war-story bank

Your battle scars are your best material because technical buyers trust pain you
have personally felt. Each story has a setup, the line, and the bridge to OGIAM.
Deploy one when the matching value prop comes up. Do not tell all four in one
meeting; pick the one that fits the buyer.

### Story A: the bad push (sells AI-code governance + the gate)
- **Setup:** "Early in my career I watched a single bad deploy take down a whole
  environment. One engineer, one push."
- **The bridge:** "Now the engineer is an AI, writing and shipping code faster
  than any human can review, and it takes actions, not just commits code. Same
  failure mode, a thousand times the speed, no audit trail."
- **The line:** "That is exactly what we gate. AI-written code gets scanned before
  it merges, and every AI action passes a deterministic check before it runs."
- **Use when:** they mention Copilot/Cursor/agents writing code, or velocity vs
  review tension.

### Story B: the region outage (sells the control plane + assurance)
- **Setup:** "I have been on the team when an entire cloud region went dark, and
  when Cloudflare went down and took half the internet with it."
- **The bridge:** "You plan for infrastructure failure. You have runbooks for it.
  Almost nobody has a runbook for AI failure, an agent doing the wrong thing at
  machine speed."
- **The line:** "We are the control plane and the flight recorder for AI actions.
  Real-time control plus a provable record, and we continuously attack our own
  gate to prove it holds."
- **Use when:** they talk resilience, reliability, or "what could go wrong with
  agents."

### Story C: the AI ontology work (sells the gap / why OGIAM exists)
- **Setup:** "I have architected full AI ontologies for enterprise teams,
  modeling the whole knowledge and agent flow."
- **The bridge:** "The piece that was always missing was governance, the
  deterministic layer that decides what the AI is actually allowed to do, and
  records it."
- **The line:** "That gap is why OGIAM exists. The intelligence is the easy part
  now. The governance is what lets you actually deploy it."
- **Use when:** they are technically deep, or already building agent
  infrastructure and think they have it handled.

### Story D: the acquisition (sells durability / why this is a company)
- **Setup:** "I took a company through an acquisition as head of engineering, so I
  have seen what an acquirer actually pays for."
- **The bridge:** "It is not the feature. It is the control point, the system of
  record, and the data that compounds."
- **The line:** "That is what we are building OGIAM to be, the durable layer under
  everyone's AI, not another wrapper."
- **Use when:** an investor or a buyer questions whether this is a feature or a
  company.

## 3. Target-account framework (mine your network first)

Warm beats cold ten to one, and your network is unusually strong. Spend the first
two weeks here before any cold outreach.

**Build the list in four buckets:**
1. **Blue Bite alumni + brand contacts.** Anyone you shipped alongside at adidas,
   eBay, Moet Hennessy, Bvlgari, and the Blue Bite team now scattered across other
   companies. Who now owns engineering, platform, or security somewhere?
2. **Consulting-era Fortune 500 contacts.** The client-side people you worked with
   on engagements. They already trust you and have budget authority.
3. **Wolfpack Agency relationships.** You are the founding tech hire at a boutique
   with deep expertise and existing client relationships. The partners' clients
   are warm intros you have not tapped yet.
4. **Cold but high-fit.** Regulated orgs (fintech, health, infra) and dev-platform
   teams visibly deploying agents. Only after the warm list is worked.

**Tier them by fit:** deploying AI agents now (Tier 1) > shipping AI features or
AI-written code (Tier 2) > "exploring AI" (Tier 3). Work Tier 1 first.

**The warm-intro ask (to a mutual contact):**
> "Quick favor. I'm building OGIAM, the control plane for AI agents, and [target]
> is exactly who should see it. Would you intro me? Happy to send a one-liner you
> can forward. No pressure if it's not a fit."

## 4. Leverage Wolfpack as a distribution channel

You are not a solo founder cold-emailing. You are the tech engine of a consulting
group with relationships and credibility. Your GTM job is partly to arm the
partners to open doors and bring you in for the technical close.

- **Equip the partners** with the demo-runbook, the one-pager, and the free-scan
  hook so they can mention OGIAM in their existing client conversations.
- **Co-sell:** the partner owns the relationship and the trust, you own the
  technical demo and the scoping. This is the same split you ran in consulting.
- **The agency's services are the wrapper:** OGIAM plus a Wolfpack governance
  engagement is a more sellable package than software alone, and it is your
  natural motion.

## 5. The weekly motion (make it a system, not a vibe)

You manage systems for a living. Manage this one. A simple weekly cadence, tracked
in a spreadsheet or a light CRM:

- **10 outreach touches/week** (warm intros first, then cold). Quality over volume.
- **3 discovery calls/week** target as the pipeline fills.
- **2 scans booked/week** is the real metric. A booked scan is the only outcome
  that matters; everything else is activity.
- **Follow up within 24 hours**, then on a 3-day / 7-day / 14-day cadence. Most
  deals are lost to silence, not to "no." This is the unsexy discipline that wins.

**Pipeline stages to track:** Contacted -> Replied -> Call booked -> Demo done ->
Scan booked -> Scan delivered -> Pilot proposed -> Closed. Move every contact one
stage per week or mark it dead. A clean pipeline is a forecast.

## 6. Role-play prep for the parts you dread

### The ask (booking the scan, end of every demo)
Say it plainly and make it small:
> "The fastest next step is to let us run the scan on one of your repos. You'll see
> your own version of that first screen, your ungoverned AI and any leaked keys, in
> a few minutes. Can you get me read access to one repo this week?"
Then stop talking. Silence is fine. Let them answer.

### The follow-up (after a demo, no reply)
> "Following up on the scan. To make it trivial, you (or your platform lead) just
> grant read access to one repo and I send back your AI surface map by [day]. Want
> me to send the access steps?"
If still silent after the 7-day touch, send a breakup note: "Sounds like the
timing isn't right, I'll close this out. If AI governance moves up the list, I'm
one reply away." Breakup notes get more replies than nagging.

### Hard objections (full set in pitch-kit.md; these are the ones you'll dread)
- **"Send me some info."** -> "Happy to, but the info won't show you your own
  ungoverned AI, the scan will, and it's faster. Can we just do the 20-minute
  readout?" (Redirect to the demo; info-sends are where deals go to die.)
- **"We'll think about it."** -> "Totally fair. What specifically do you want to
  be sure of? If it's whether this is real, the scan settles that in minutes. If
  it's budget or timing, tell me and I'll work around it." (Surface the real
  objection; "think about it" is never the real one.)
- **"We don't have budget for this."** -> "The scan is free and the readout is
  free, so let's start there and put a real risk number in front of whoever owns
  the budget. Governance gets funded when the gap is visible, not before."
- **"Won't Microsoft build this?"** -> the neutral-system-of-record answer from
  pitch-kit.md.

### The mindset cue for all of it
You are diagnosing, not convincing. You did this for a decade in consulting. The
prospect has a problem; you are the doctor who can see it. That posture removes
the neediness that makes selling feel gross.

## 7. The 30-day ramp (build the muscle with reps)

- **Week 1:** Build the target list (all four buckets). Write 5 warm-intro asks.
  Do 2 practice demos with friendly contacts. Rehearse the runbook twice.
- **Week 2:** Send 10 warm touches. Run 3 demos (mix of friendly + real). Book your
  first scan. Record yourself once and tighten the narration.
- **Week 3:** 10 more touches. 3 demos. 2 scans booked. Deliver your first scan
  readout and watch the prospect's reaction (this is your real product feedback).
- **Week 4:** Convert one scan into a pilot conversation using the $60k bundled
  offer. Turn the first delivered scan into a mini case study (anonymized if
  needed). Use it in the next round of outreach.

By day 30 the discomfort is mostly gone, you have a repeatable motion, and you have
real market signal that tells you what to build and price next. That signal is
worth more than any feature you could have built in the same month.
